# Completion & Evidence Expectations

**Purpose of this page:** enumerate what "done" means and what to file — per official notification, never assumed. Verify every item below against your department's current circular; sessions vary.

## 1. What Are We Doing, and Why

Universities convert MOOC effort into records (credits/marks/eligibility) only through notified evidence. The chain: enroll (approved listing) → progress (graded items on time) → certificate/exam (as notified) → submit proof (department channel) → reflect in records. One missing link voids the rest — track the chain, not just the learning.

## 2. Concept in Very Simple Language

- **Completion evidence:** whatever the notification names — typically the platform certificate and/or proctored-exam score. Screenshots of progress pages are personal tracking, not proof, unless explicitly accepted.
- **Proctored exam:** supervised (centre or remote-proctored) final assessment on notified dates, with ID and fee formalities weeks earlier — registration windows close silently.
- **Credit mapping:** how the institution counts the MOOC (audit/pass-fail/graded credit) — decides how much effort optimisation is rational, and it's notification-specific.

## 3. Evidence Checklist (Verify Each Against the Circular)

1. Approved course name + session + platform link (screenshot the notification itself).
2. Enrollment confirmation under your registered name/ID (typos here orphan certificates).
3. Weekly graded-item completions (log dates; platform history is the backup).
4. Exam registration + fee receipt + hall ticket / remote-proctoring readiness check.
5. Final certificate/score + department submission acknowledgment (the chain's last link — chase it in writing).

::: callout-pitfall Name-Mismatch Orphans
Certificates issued to nicknames, wrong spellings, or personal (not registered) emails routinely fail verification. Match the institution's records character-for-character at enrollment — corrections later are slow or impossible.
:::

::: toggle What are "proctored exams", "credit mapping", and "submission acknowledgment"?
Proctored exam = supervised final assessment (exam centre or remote-proctored with camera/screen monitoring), on notified dates, usually needing separate registration plus fee weeks earlier. Credit mapping = how your institution counts the MOOC (audit, pass/fail, or graded credit — decides rational effort and follows the specific notification). Submission acknowledgment = written confirmation the department received your proof (email reply, stamped receipt — chase it; unacknowledged submissions are unfiled evidence).
:::

**Watch out:** (1) Assuming auto-credit (nothing transfers without the notified submission step). (2) Missing fee/exam-registration windows (learning without the exam sitting = incomplete where exams apply). (3) Trusting memory over filed acknowledgments (get submission receipts in writing).

**Active-recall checklist:** Notification screenshot filed? Name matched? Exam registered? Submission acknowledged in writing?

## 4. Active Recall Quizzes

::: quiz Certificate earned, but records show no credit. The student never submitted anything to the department. What broke and what was the missing link?
() Certificates auto-transfer; the office erred
(*) The submission step: credit needs the notified handoff (certificate + channel + acknowledgment). Learning complete, chain incomplete — evidence unfiled is evidence absent
() Certificates are decorative and worthless
() Departments reject all MOOCs on principle
::: explanation
Chains have last links: enroll→progress→certificate→submit→record. Celebrating link three while skipping link four is the classic incomplete-completion — file and chase acknowledgment.
:::

::: quiz Proctored exam needs fee payment and ID verification weeks ahead. Why must these be calendared in week one?
() Bureaucracy enjoys early paperwork
(*) Registration windows close silently and late requests are refused — eligibility is time-boxed, not merit-based at the gate. Week-one calendaring converts surprise into routine
() Exams are first-come-first-served by seats
() Week-one effort impresses evaluators
::: explanation
Gates close on calendars, not on readiness: eligible-but-unregistered sits nothing. External timelines ignore your semester rhythm — import them into your calendar before they import consequences.
:::
