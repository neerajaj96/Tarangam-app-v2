# Revision: Pointers Only, No Syllabus

**Purpose of this page:** teach *how to revise* for the MOOC's assessments using only official material — with zero constitutional claims of our own. This page contains method, not matter.

## 1. What Are We Doing, and Why

Proctored MOOC exams reward revisiting official videos, transcripts, and readings — not third-party summaries of unknown fidelity. The method: syllabus-map your notes to the MOOC's stated week-outcomes, drill its own quizzes/assignments, and rehearse explaining each outcome aloud. Gaps found → rewatch the official segment, never invent the answer.

## 2. Concept in Very Simple Language

- **Official sources only:** the MOOC's videos, transcripts, readings, and the constitutional text it cites. Anything else is unverified until cross-checked against these.
- **Outcome-mapped revision:** list each week's stated outcomes; mark confident/shaky per outcome; rewatch shaky ones fully (outcomes are the exam's blueprint).
- **Anti-fabrication rule:** never write or memorise constitutional "facts" from memory, seniors, or random sites — a wrong article number memorised confidently still scores zero and misleads. Verify-then-memorise, always.

## 3. Revision Protocol (Numbered)

1. Export/list the MOOC's week-wise outcomes (copy exactly — paraphrase loses testable nouns).
2. Self-rate each outcome (confident/shaky/blank) in one pass without notes.
3. Rewatch + re-read all shaky/blanks from official material only; correct your notes with source timestamps.
4. Redo the MOOC's own quizzes closed-book; log every miss with its official correction.
5. Explain three random outcomes aloud (or to a peer) — fluency gaps are exam gaps.

::: callout-pitfall Summary Worship
Polished third-party summaries feel efficient and test nothing reliably — fidelity unknown, outcomes unmapped. Summaries may *index* revision (what to rewatch), never *replace* official segments.
:::

**Watch out:** (1) Memorising article numbers from unverified lists (verify-then-memorise). (2) Skipping transcripts (searchable text beats rewatching hours for targeted gaps). (3) No closed-book rehearsal (recognition ≠ recall — the exam tests recall).

**Active-recall checklist:** Outcomes listed verbatim? Self-rated honestly? Shaky ones rewatched officially? Closed-book redo logged? Three explained aloud?

## 4. Active Recall Quizzes

::: quiz A senior's "complete notes" PDF disagrees with the MOOC transcript on an article's content. Which wins and what is the rule?
() Senior notes — seniors never err
(*) The official transcript (and the constitutional text it cites): verify-then-memorise. Third-party material indexes revision but never overrules official sources — fidelity decides, not seniority
() Newer document always wins regardless
() Both are equally valid; pick either
::: explanation
Authority hierarchy: constitution > MOOC official material > everything else. Disagreements resolve upward, never by convenience — the anti-fabrication rule in one decision.
:::

::: quiz Why must revision map to the MOOC's stated week-outcomes instead of just rewatching everything?
() Outcomes are decorative metadata
(*) Outcomes are the exam's blueprint: they enumerate what's testable, so self-rating per outcome targets effort at shaky items. Blind rewatching spends equal time on mastered and missing — mapping converts hours into coverage
() Rewatching everything is faster
() Exams ignore stated outcomes entirely
::: explanation
Blueprint-driven effort: list, rate, target, verify. Coverage is measured against outcomes, not hours — the protocol turns revision from ritual into evidence.
:::
