# Using the External MOOC Well

**Purpose of this page:** a weekly routine for completing the official external MOOC reliably. All constitutional learning happens there; this page only organises your effort.

## 1. What Are We Doing, and Why

MOOCs fail by drift (week-6 pileups) not difficulty. The routine: fixed weekly slot, active watching (pause-summarise per video), same-week quiz attempts, one running notes page. Small steady doses beat deadline heroics — platforms lock late submissions without appeal.

## 2. Concept in Very Simple Language

- **Active watching:** pause every 5–10 minutes, restate the point aloud in one line, resume — passivity is the MOOC dropout mechanism.
- **Same-week quizzes:** attempt while memory is warm; re-attempt where the platform allows (read the rules — some average, some take best).
- **Running notes page:** one document, dated entries, key terms + one-line meanings + doubts list for discussion forums.
- **Separation rule:** Tarangam guide pages answer "how do I complete this?"; MOOC videos/texts answer every "what does the constitution say?" — never mix the citations.

## 3. Weekly Routine (Numbered)

1. Open the week's module; note its stated outcomes first (aim before content).
2. Watch actively (pause-summarise), logging terms + doubts.
3. Attempt the week's quiz same-week; log wrong answers with corrections.
4. Post one doubt or answer one peer question in the forum (participation deepens retention and sometimes counts).
5. File the week's certificate-progress screenshot/statement where your records live (evidence hygiene from day one).

::: callout-pitfall Deadline Amnesia
External MOOC deadlines ignore college exam seasons — calendar them in week one with two reminders each (one week, one day before). "The site was closed" has never been accepted as an excuse anywhere.
:::

**Watch out:** (1) Binge-watching without quizzes (views ≠ completion — only graded items count). (2) Sharing quiz answers as "help" (platforms treat it as malpractice; discuss concepts, never copy answers). (3) Skipping forums entirely (peer explanation is the cheapest revision available).

**Active-recall checklist:** Weekly slot fixed? Pause-summarise habit? Same-week quizzes? Evidence filed?

## 4. Active Recall Quizzes

::: quiz A student watches all videos in the final weekend and fails the completion criteria. What exactly went wrong?
() Videos don't count as learning scientifically
(*) Graded items (quizzes/assignments with deadlines) were missed or rushed — views alone rarely satisfy completion; steady same-week attempts plus filed evidence is the routine that passes
() Weekends are banned on MOOC platforms
() Completion is random and blameless
::: explanation
Completion = graded artefacts on time, not hours watched. The routine converts watching into artefacts weekly — bingeing converts it into regret.
:::

::: quiz Forum asks for last week's quiz answers and a classmate posts them. Why must you discuss concepts instead?
() Forums are read-only by design
(*) Posted answers are platform malpractice (penalties up to disqualification); concept discussion teaches without copying. Help peers understand, never hand over responses
() Quiz answers are copyrighted state secrets
() Nobody reads forums anyway
::: explanation
Integrity boundary: explain the idea in your own words, solve your own items. The distinction protects both giver and receiver — generosity stays inside it.
:::
